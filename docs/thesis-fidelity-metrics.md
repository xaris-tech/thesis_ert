# Thesis text — reconstruction fidelity of the saline phantom

Paste-ready Methods and Results blocks. Terminology follows `CONTEXT.md`; the phantom
work is an *evidential reconstruction*, and the target is a *plastic phantom target*.

Three numbers are used throughout: **angular localization error**, **NCC**, and **Dice**.
Two conventions are load-bearing and should not be dropped when this is edited:

1. Every score is reported as a **margin** — detection minus its own empty-tank control —
   never as a raw score.
2. The angle is reported in **degrees**, never as a percentage.

---

## Methods

### Assessing localization against a known contrast target

Localization was assessed by placing the plastic phantom target at known positions in the
saline phantom and measuring where the reconstruction placed it. Twenty-four block
placements were scored: twelve single positions distributed around the electrode ring, one
at the center, and six diametrically opposed pairs. Each placement was imaged as an
evidential reconstruction, differenced against a baseline scan of the same phantom with no
target present.

Three metrics were computed per scan. Each was also computed on a **control image**, built
by differencing the baseline scan against itself and therefore containing instrumental
noise but no target. Reported quantities are **margins** — detection score minus the
corresponding control score — because an absolute score is uninterpretable without its
null.

This was not a formality. An earlier formulation of the structural metric averaged over
the whole tank, in which the target mask occupies about one per cent of the image area, and
consequently returned 0.90–0.96 for an empty tank while every genuine detection scored
0.28–0.59. A verification procedure without a null would have declared the instrument
working.

**Angular localization error.** The angular displacement, in degrees, between the recorded
position of the target and the reconstructed conductivity variation pattern. The pattern's
position was taken as the centre of mass of its connected lobe, thresholded at half that
lobe's own peak, rather than as the single strongest pixel. The single-pixel estimate is
biased by artefacts at the electrode ring and reported one placement as 18.2° from the
true bearing where the lobe centroid placed it 4.9°; across the series the single-pixel
estimate produced a maximum error of 18.2° against 11.9° for the lobe centroid.

The lobe is the connected component containing the strongest pixel, so a compact artefact
brighter than the target would itself become the lobe. No centroid estimator avoids this,
because the artefact is by definition the strongest signal; the single-pixel estimate is
therefore reported alongside the lobe estimate so that a disagreement between them stays
visible.

Ground truth for this metric is **photographic**: a top-down photograph of the tank with
the target in place was taken for 18 of the 19 scored scans, and the recorded placement
was confirmed against it. Localization is claimed only to the precision these photographs
support — a few degrees. Because the target's radial position was not measured with a rule
in any photograph, no claim is made about radial accuracy. The 22 placements from
photographed scans form the reported localization set; the two from the unphotographed
scan are discussed under limitations.

**NCC.** The Pearson correlation between the reconstructed image and an expected image
generated by simulating a block of the measured 23 × 22 mm footprint at the recorded
position and reconstructing it through the identical solver. NCC is invariant to overall
scale and sensitive to position, so a correct but faint pattern is not penalised while a
pattern in the wrong place scores near zero.

**Dice.** The overlap of the two thresholded patterns, 2|A∩B| / (|A| + |B|), both
thresholded at half their peak. Dice is reported because it is directly interpretable as
the fraction of the claimed target region that coincides with the expected region.

Neither NCC nor Dice may be read as a similarity index in the structural-similarity sense.
Both are computed against a template produced by the same reconstruction pipeline, so they
measure the **repeatability** of that pipeline, not its correctness: a systematic bias would
move the reconstruction and the template together and would not appear in either score.

## Results

**Localization.** Across the 22 block placements with photographic ground truth, the
absolute angular localization error had a median of **3.4°** and a maximum of **11.9°**; 16
of 22 placements fell within 5° and 21 of 22 within 10°, where 10° is one third of the 30°
angular spacing between adjacent electrodes. The single placement outside 10° was 11.9°, in
the scan acquired nearest to E2, which is also the sector at which baseline drift appeared
throughout that session.

For completeness, the two placements from the scan without photographic ground truth
returned 0.4° and 1.0°. Including them would improve every summary figure — the median
would fall from 3.4° to 3.1° and the count within 10° would rise from 21 of 22 to 23 of 24
— which is precisely why they are excluded rather than quietly pooled: that scan's
recorded sector was reconstructed from its own reconstruction, so its error is measured
against the quantity being measured.

**Repeatability.** Every scored scan exceeded its own control on both metrics: **19 of 19**
for NCC and **19 of 19** for Dice. Median margins were **0.60** for NCC (range 0.21 to
1.39) and **0.76** for Dice (range 0.16 to 0.98).

Two scans carried margins far below the rest. Scan `145332` had an NCC margin of 0.21,
its control image itself scoring 0.69 against the E7 template, which indicates that this
baseline already contained structure near E7 before any target was introduced — consistent
with either baseline contamination or session drift. Scan `174738` had a Dice margin of
0.16. Both are candidates for repeat measurement.

## Discussion

The three metrics answer different questions and are not interchangeable. The angular
localization error is the only one whose ground truth was independent of the reconstruction
software, and it is therefore the localization result. NCC and Dice demonstrate that the
reconstruction is repeatable and that its recovered region coincides with the expected
region, which is necessary for any later comparison but would not, on its own, reveal a
systematic error in the reconstruction.

The distinction is not academic. The prototype's own measurements show that the structural
metric originally adopted cannot distinguish signal from noise in this setting, and the
angular estimate changed by more than 6° for one placement depending only on how the
pattern's centre was defined. Neither failure would have been visible without a control
image and an independent ground truth.

Four limitations bound these results.

**Radial accuracy is not established.** The target's radial position was recorded but not
measured photographically, so the reconstruction's radial response could not be verified
against it. Its radial behaviour was separately checked against simulated targets and found
consistent over the range tested, but no radial claim is made here.

**Depth sensitivity was not tested.** Every placement was at a single depth, so the results
establish lateral localization only.

**Specificity was not tested.** Nothing here demonstrates that the metrics would fail to
respond in the absence of a target; the controls bound that response only for the specific
baselines recorded.

**One placement lacks photographic ground truth.** Scan `173951` was typed at capture as a
pair inconsistent with its planned sector and relabelled afterwards from its own
reconstruction, and no photograph of that setup was taken. Its two placements are excluded
from the localization result above, and are reported separately in the results rather than
pooled.
